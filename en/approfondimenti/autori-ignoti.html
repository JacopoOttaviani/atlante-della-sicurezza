    How many crimes remain without a known offender

    For most crimes reported in Italy nobody knows who did it, and where the offender remains unknown a trial does not even begin. A known offender, for that matter, is not a guilty one: it is the person named to the judiciary, which will decide.

          Method and sources

          How to read these numbers

          “Known offender” means that someone was identified by the end of the year, not that they are guilty: people identified later are not counted, and crimes nobody reports are not included. A high share can also reflect how much checking goes on, as with drug dealing and handling stolen goods.

          How the numbers were built, and sources

          What “known offender” means

          These are the crimes committed in the year and reported by the police to the judicial authorities for which the offender, or at least one of the offenders, is known: because the victim named them, because they were caught in the act or because investigators identified them by the end of the year. The share is a first tally, lower than the final one; a crime with several offenders counts once.

          Known offender does not mean guilty

          The person named as the offender has been reported or arrested, not convicted: under the Constitution they are not considered guilty until the final judgment, and the trial may acquit them. These numbers measure how many crimes reach the judiciary with a name attached, not how many are punished.

          Cities and provinces

          The breakdown by type of crime exists only for the twelve municipalities with the most residents; for the provinces ISTAT publishes the share for all crimes combined. For the cities the crimes of the last three years are added together; for the provinces the shares for the same years are averaged. In Sardegna ISTAT counts crimes in the four historical provinces: the map colours them that way, on today’s boundaries.
