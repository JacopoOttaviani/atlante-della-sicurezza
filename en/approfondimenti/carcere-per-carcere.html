    Prison by prison

    Italy’s prisons hold more people than they have places, and those who count deaths in prison do not always arrive at the same number. Prison by prison, month by month since 2019.

    By Jacopo Ottaviani · Updated 21 October 2026

    The picture

    People in prison and places

    Two ways of counting places

    Official capacity (capienza regolamentare), published every month by the Ministry, follows the standard for homes fit to live in: nine square metres for one person alone in a cell, five more for each additional person. It includes cells that cannot be used.

    Places actually available (posti regolarmente disponibili) leave out cells and wings closed for works, damage or because they are unfit for use. The National Guarantor for the rights of persons deprived of liberty publishes them a few times a year and calculates overcrowding on them.

      If you need to talk

      If you are thinking about suicide, or are worried about someone, in Italy you can call Telefono Amico Italia on 02 2327 2327, every day. In an emergency call 112. Elsewhere, findahelpline.com lists free helplines by country.

      This page counts deaths and says which prison they are recorded under, not how: that is what the World Health Organization’s guidelines recommend to anyone writing about suicide.

        What we don’t know

        How many people share each cell, and how much space they have: the tables stop at the level of the prison, and a half-empty prison can have overcrowded wings. How many places are available each month, prison by prison. The legal status of people in each prison, which would be needed to understand the case of Friuli-Venezia Giulia. How many people die shortly after leaving prison, or in hospital weeks after a suicide attempt. How many of the deaths “still to be established” are suicides. And why a person takes their own life: the numbers do not say, and this page does not try to.

        How the numbers were built, and sources

        Why this page

        The rest of the atlas counts reported crimes; this page looks inside prisons. The approach is that of The Marshall Project, the American newsroom that covers criminal justice: official data read to the end, the method next to the numbers, and words that do not reduce people to their detention. That is why this page says “people in prison”, not “prisoners” or “inmates”.

        The Ministry’s tables

        Every month the Ministry of Justice publishes, from data of the prison administration department (DAP), a table of every adult prison: type, official capacity, people present on the last day of the month, women and foreign nationals. All the tables since January 2019 are used here (December 2021 was published twice, identically), and the script checks that each month the rows add up to the total. At the end of 2025 the figures by region match those ISTAT publishes from the same source, used on the page on pre-trial detention.

        Prisons over time and on the map

        Prison names change from one table to the next: the script recognises prisons by region and name, with a short list of old and new names. Sanremo went from a prison for sentenced people to a remand prison in 2022. Camerino, empty, drops out of the tables in July 2019; Pozzuoli, for women, is empty; the centre at Gjadër, in Albania, is listed among the prisons of Rome from April to September 2025 and again from September 2026, always with nobody held there: it is not on the map, but its places count in the national capacity. Each prison is placed at the central point of its municipality, not at its exact address.

        How the data were built

        The Ministry’s tables are web pages, read by a script. The Guarantor’s are published only as PDFs: they were copied one by one, with the page they come from, into scripts/istituti_tabelle.json, together with the counts by Ristretti Orizzonti and Antigone. The script re-checks the sums and stops if a row of the Guarantor’s tables does not find its prison or the capacity does not match. All the numbers on this page are in data/istituti.json.
