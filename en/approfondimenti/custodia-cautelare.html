    Deep dives · Pre-trial detention

    Before the verdict

    Under the Italian Constitution, someone awaiting a final judgment is not considered guilty. How many people are in prison in that position, where, and how much the State pays when custody turns out to be unjust.

    By Jacopo Ottaviani · Updated 21 October 2026

    PrisonsReturnsPerception and realityUnknown offendersMinorsPre-trial detentionPrison by prison
    The overall picture

    People in prison without a final conviction

    What pre-trial custody is

    It is not a punishment: a judge orders it before the verdict when there is serious evidence and a concrete risk that the person will flee, tamper with evidence or commit further crimes. Prison is the last of the possible measures, after reporting to the police, a ban on living in a place and house arrest.

    On this page we write “people in prison” and “awaiting trial”, not “inmates” and “defendants”: many of the people counted here will be convicted, others will not.

        When custody turns out to be unjust, and how much the State pays

        Anyone held in pre-trial custody and then acquitted, or whose case was dropped, or who was detained under an unlawful order, can ask the court of appeal for fair compensation: up to €516,456.90, paid by the Ministry of Economy and Finance.

          Method and sources

          What we don’t know

          How long pre-trial custody lasts, and how many people it involves rather than how many measures: there are no public statistics. How many people held in custody are acquitted at the end of a long trial: the report follows only proceedings closed within the year. How many would be entitled to compensation and do not ask for it.

          How the numbers were built, and sources

          Why this page

          The rest of the atlas counts reported crimes; this page looks at what happens next. The approach is that of The Marshall Project, the American newsroom that covers criminal justice: official data read to the end, the method next to the numbers, and words that do not reduce people to their trial.

          Without a final conviction

          These are the people held in adult prisons on 31 December (ISTAT, from data of the Department of Prison Administration) who are awaiting first trial, appealing, awaiting the Court of Cassation, or in mixed positions with no final conviction: the total the prison administration calls “imputati”, the same used in the deep dive on prisons. People with at least one final conviction and other proceedings open count as convicted.

          A snapshot at the end of the year

          The numbers count who is in prison on 31 December, not how many people went through pre-trial custody during the year, nor for how long. The region is where the prison is. People under house arrest and minors are not included.

          Precautionary measures

          They come from the report the Government presents to Parliament every year (Law 47 of 2015, Article 15), which since 2017 has also covered compensation. In 2024, 84% of offices responded and the Ministry estimated the rest; 2018 and 2019 also contain estimates. The report counts measures, not people.

          Why 2025 is missing

          The report of 30 December 2025 (Doc. XCIV no. 4) covers January to October 2025, with 57% of offices responding and no estimates, and itself warns against comparing those numbers with earlier years. So measures and outcomes stop at 2024; from that report we use only the disciplinary proceedings, updated to November 2025, and the requests for compensation. Payments for 2025 were not yet available.

          Compensation

          Orders and amounts are those received by the Ministry of Economy during the year, by court of appeal, and can concern custody from many years earlier. The detached sections of Sassari, Taranto and Bolzano are counted separately. Residents are the provinces of each district on 1 January 2025, with approximate boundaries where a court has municipalities in two provinces: Massa-Carrara is with Genoa; the province of Nuoro is counted entirely with Sassari, though on the map Ogliastra (the Lanusei court) is with Cagliari.

          How the data were built

          The report’s tables are published only as PDFs: they were transcribed one by one, with the page they come from, into scripts/cautelari_relazione.json. The script checks that measures by type add up to the total, that the districts add up to the national totals, and that amounts divided by orders match the averages published by the Ministry. All the numbers on this page are in data/cautelari.json.

          Sources

            The reports (in Italian): 2024 data, January 2025 edition (Ministry of Justice) and year 2025, Doc. XCIV no. 4 (Chamber of Deputies). For the method of the whole atlas: Methodology and sources.
