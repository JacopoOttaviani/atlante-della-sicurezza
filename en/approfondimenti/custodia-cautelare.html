    Before the verdict

    How many people are in prison without a final conviction, how many precautionary measures the courts order, and how much the State pays when custody turns out to have been unjust. Someone awaiting trial is neither guilty nor innocent: under the Italian Constitution they are not considered guilty until the final judgment.

    People in prison without a final conviction

    The words we use

    On this page we write “people in prison”, not “prisoners” or “inmates” as a category, and “awaiting trial”, not “defendants” as if it were a personal trait. Someone awaiting a final judgment is not considered guilty (Article 27 of the Italian Constitution), and some of the people counted here will be convicted, others will not.

    Pre-trial custody is not a punishment: a judge orders it before the verdict when there is serious evidence and a concrete risk that the person will flee, tamper with evidence or commit further crimes. Prison is the last of the possible measures, after reporting to the police, a ban on living in a place and house arrest.

          Method and sources

          How to read these numbers

          Why this page

          The rest of the atlas counts reported crimes. This page looks at what happens next: the system that decides who stays in prison while waiting for a verdict, and what happens when that decision turns out to be wrong. The approach is that of The Marshall Project, the American newsroom that covers criminal justice: official data read to the end, the method published next to the numbers, what the data cannot say written out plainly, and words that do not reduce people to their trial.

          Without a final conviction

          ISTAT publishes, from the data of the Department of Prison Administration (DAP), the people held in adult prisons on 31 December of each year, by legal status. “Without a final conviction” adds up four groups: awaiting first trial, appellants (convicted at first instance, awaiting appeal), people awaiting the Court of Cassation, and mixed positions with no final conviction (several proceedings, none closed). It is the same total that the prison administration calls “imputati”, and the same used in the deep dive on prisons. People in “mixed positions with a final conviction”, who have at least one final conviction and other proceedings still open, count as convicted.

          A snapshot at the end of the year

          The numbers count who is in prison on 31 December, not how many people went through pre-trial custody during the year, nor how long they stayed. The region is the one where the prison is. People under house arrest are not included, nor are minors, who are held in separate institutions.

          Precautionary measures

          Since 2015 the Government must present Parliament every year with a report on personal precautionary measures (Law 47 of 2015, Article 15) and, since 2017, on compensation for wrongful detention. The data come from the preliminary investigations judges and the trial sections of the courts: in 2024, 84% of offices responded and the Ministry estimated the missing ones; 2018 and 2019 also contain estimates. The report counts measures, not people, and only follows the outcome of proceedings that ended in the same year in which the measure was ordered.

          Why 2025 is missing

          The report sent to the Chamber of Deputies on 30 December 2025 (Doc. XCIV no. 4) contains the measures from January to October 2025, with 57% of offices responding and no estimates for the missing ones: the report itself says so, and warns against comparing them with earlier years. For this reason measures and outcomes here stop at 2024. From that report we only use the disciplinary proceedings, updated to November 2025. Payments for 2025 were not there yet: the Ministry of Economy had not sent them.

          Compensation

          The orders and amounts are those received by the Ministry of Economy during the year, by court of appeal: they concern custody from earlier years, sometimes many years earlier. The three detached sections (Sassari, Taranto, Bolzano) are counted separately, as in the report. For residents we add up the provinces of each district on 1 January 2025; a few courts have municipalities in two provinces, and there the boundary is approximate. Massa-Carrara is in the Genoa district. In Sardinia the province of Nuoro, which includes Ogliastra (the Lanusei court, Cagliari district), is counted entirely with Sassari; on the map Ogliastra is with Cagliari.

          What we don’t know

          How long pre-trial custody lasts: there is no public statistic on its duration, nor on the number of people rather than measures. How many people placed in custody are acquitted at the end of a long trial: the report only follows proceedings closed within the year. How many people would be entitled to compensation and do not ask for it: the request must be made within two years of the final judgment, and anyone who contributed to their own detention through intent or gross negligence has no right to it.

          How the data were built

          The report’s tables are published only as PDFs: they were transcribed one by one, with the page they come from, into the file scripts/cautelari_relazione.json. The script that builds the data checks that the measures by type add up to the total, that the districts’ amounts and orders add up to the national totals for each year, and that amounts divided by orders give the averages published by the Ministry, district by district. The file with all the numbers on this page is data/cautelari.json.
