    Method and sources

    How to read these numbers

    Two different questions on perception

    ISTAT’s Aspects of Daily Life survey asks households whether the area where they live is very, fairly, not very or not at all at risk of crime: this is the figure for the big cities and the provinces. The European EU-SILC survey asks whether there are problems of crime, violence or vandalism in the area: this is the figure for the long national series. They are different questions, which is why the levels do not match.

    Reported crimes are not all crimes

    Reported crimes are those the police have passed on to the judicial authorities. Many crimes are never reported, and the propensity to report them varies from crime to crime and from place to place: reported crimes are therefore not the whole “reality”, but the part of reality that official data manage to count.

    The rate is calculated on residents

    In cities that take in commuters, students and tourists every day, crimes also affect people who do not live there, so the rate per resident comes out higher than the real likelihood of being a victim for those who do. Fear, on the other hand, is reported by residents.

    The provincial estimates

    Perception by province comes from a sample survey: in small provinces few households are interviewed and the estimate swings from one year to the next. This is why the chart uses the average of the available years, for perception and for reported crimes alike. In Sardegna, crimes follow the four historical provinces, as in the rest of the atlas.
