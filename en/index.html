          Spikes and falls

          Where something changed suddenly

          A spike is a year in which reports of a crime suddenly jump: at least a quarter above the average of the previous three years, with at least 20 cases and a jump that chance alone does not explain. “In proportion” puts the most solid increases first, “By extra cases” the ones that affect the most people.           Beyond the reports

          Nine questions that reported crimes alone cannot answer: who is in prison, how many returns are actually carried out, how far fear follows crime, for how many crimes the offender is known, what share of the people reported are minors, how many people are in prison before a final conviction, how full prisons are and how many people die in them, how long trials last and how many end because time runs out, and how many people serve their sentence outside prison. Each one has its own page, and each card shows which stage its numbers come from: the police report, the time before the verdict, the trial, prison or sentences served outside it. A police report is not a conviction. All the deep dives.
